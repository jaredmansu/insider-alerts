04 / Open methodology

No mystery score.
No promised returns.

The grade is an attention filter. The calculation is public so you can see exactly what it rewards—and what it discounts.

A 7+B 5–6C 3–4D ≤2
How the points add up
Role (highest applicable)
CEO, CFO, President, or Chair +3; other officer or director +2; 10% owner +1.
Transaction value
At least $1 million +2; otherwise at least $250,000 +1.
Stake change
At least 25%, or a new purchase position, +2; otherwise at least 10% +1.
Cluster buying
Two or more insiders buying the same ticker in seven days +2.
Purchase history
+1 for no prior buys in at least five checked historical filings, or for at least two prior buys. Applied only to purchases when history is available.
Qualifiers
A disclosed Rule 10b5-1 plan −2. An option exercise paired with a same-day sale −3.

History may arrive after the initial alert and update its grade. Sales can receive high grades too: a grade is not a bullish or bearish recommendation.

What the data can—and can’t—tell you

“First-time-buyer” means first recorded in our database, which started in August 2026. The premium history check examines up to ten recent Form 4 filings; it is not a complete lifetime record.

Quotes are best effort, cached for ten minutes, and may be delayed or unavailable. Filings can be late or contain errors. The bot watches Form 4 filings, not amendments (Form 4/A), so always check the source.

Why a filing alert isn’t a real-time trade alert

An alert starts with a public filing, not a live brokerage transaction. Most reportable insider transactions must be filed within two business days, and some arrive later. Each alert includes the trade date and filing delay.

The bot only posts new filings that meet its filters. Quiet periods, weekends, and holidays can have no qualifying alerts. Service interruptions can also delay delivery; timing is not guaranteed.
